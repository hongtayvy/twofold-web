Taking the name literally.

Two readings of TwoFold. The first three build the numeral out of folded paper. The last three fold the money itself. Both still carry the two-tone partner story.

Display size, nav size beside the wordmark, 20px, and reversed on dark.

A two, made of folds

Ribbon Two

Recommended

One strip of paper folded twice until it becomes a 2. The name is in the drawing rather than beside it: two, and folds, in a single object. The hairline breaks are the creases.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Folded Two

The same numeral creased once across the middle. Quieter than Ribbon Two and easier to read at a glance, because the digit stays whole and the fold is a single event.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Halved Two

Split down the middle instead of across. Less about folding, more about two people holding the same number. The strongest of the three if you want the digit to stay obvious at tiny sizes.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Double Fold

A strip folded twice, shown edge on. The most abstract of the literal set and the most energetic. Honest risk: without context it can read as mountains rather than paper.

Money, folded

Note Fold

A bill folded down the middle and stood open, one panel each. Banknote proportions keep it from reading as a book. The friendliest way to say "your money, shared" without drawing a dollar sign.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Creased Note

A bill creased across, the top panel tipping toward you. Reads as a real object rather than an icon, and the two panels give each partner a half without splitting anything in an awkward place.

One caution on the numeral marks

A logo that is just a digit is harder to own than a shape. Numerals are common marks and they are weaker in trademark terms, so whichever you like, run a clearance check before it goes anywhere real. The folded-money set has more room to be distinctly yours.
