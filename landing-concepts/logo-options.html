Six ways out of the Mastercard problem.

The old mark was two equal circles overlapping in two colours, which is the Mastercard construction and unusable for a finance product. Each option below keeps the two-tone partner story and drops the Venn.

Every mark is shown at display size, at nav size beside the wordmark, at 20px, and reversed on dark.

The Crease

Recommended

An open fold seen from above, two panels meeting at a centre crease. The most literal read of the name, and it carries a second meaning: two sides of one thing, hinged together.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Split

Safest

One circle, two halves, a hairline gap. Because it is a single circle rather than two, the payments association disappears completely. Scales further down than anything else here.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Turned Corner

The fold, stated plainly. Warm and tactile, and it hints at paper rather than software. The risk is honest: at small sizes it can read as a generic document or notes icon.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Quarter

One rounded form with a single quadrant in the second tone. Reads as one thing made of two shares, which is closer to how couples actually split money than a fifty-fifty mark is.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Offset

Two rounded bars, aligned but not identical. The calmest and most systematic of the six. Its weakness is real: at favicon size it starts to look like a menu icon.

TwoFold 20px on dark

Fold Arc

The same square, divided by a soft crease instead of a straight edge. The warmest option and the least corporate, which suits the no-lectures tone. Softest at very small sizes.

TwoFold 20px on dark

The seventh option: no mark at all.

Plenty of finance products run on a wordmark alone and let the product screens carry the colour. If none of the six earn their place, this is a legitimate answer rather than a failure, and it removes a thing to get wrong.
